# What changed in the revision

Revision of 11 November 2026, following `improvement_plan.md`. The aim was a report that is easier to follow, with simpler methods and figures that read at first glance.

- **Old version:** `paper/archive/` (`report_v1_before_revision.pdf`, `main_v1_before_revision.pdf`, and the old `main.tex` and `numbers.tex`).
- **New version:** `paper/report.pdf` (front page + 5 pages; the old one had 6).

## 1. Method: one simple rule instead of a model

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Three z-scored scores (identity, production, demand), combined by a Gaussian mixture model | **One rule:** a name is ghost-like if it lacks at least 4 of 5 identity markers (verified, bio, link, gallery photo, "This Is" playlist) |
| A second, production-free label for the ownership figure | Gone; the rule already uses no production data |
| Spearman correlations between the scores as the main check | Gone. Replaced by Table 1: ghost-like vs real names on the same playlists, in plain units (house size, followers per listener, credits) |
| Hand-label sheet and kappa script | Removed (`notes/hand_label_sheet.csv`, `src/features/kappa.py`, README section) |

**Why:** the mixture collapsed onto one value of the production score and called 373 names with no identity "real". The rule also removes the circularity: house and listening patterns are now findings, not inputs.

**Effect on the headline:** ghost-like names now hold 75% of mood-playlist slots, up from 65%. Firefly is flagged at 90% (was 94%). Classical majors are flagged at 18% (was 1%); the paper states this as the rule's main error.

## 2. Analysis changes

- **One house definition everywhere (E7).** A name's house is the registrant of most of its primary tracks. A house's size is the number of names whose house it is. Before, three different sizes were in use.
- **Label-free concentration.** The eight largest houses registered 72% of mood-playlist slots that have a code, against 22% on genre playlists. No flag is involved (Fig. 3B and Results section 2).
- **Recommender.** The output now stores the largest component (4,620 names; the old caption wrongly said 4,827, E1) and the null models' 95% range. The z-scores are dropped from the paper (E9).
- **Audio analysis** now scores the stored sample with the current flag, not the flag it was drawn with.
- **Text analysis** adds a bio-length baseline (AUC 0.80 from length alone, against 0.89 for the full classifier).
- **New helper** `src/figures/houses.py`: the eight houses are numbered and named the same way in Figs. 1 and 3.

All numbers are regenerated by the pipeline. `paper/numbers.tex` has 114 macros, rebuilt from scratch, with the old score, correlation and two-label macros removed.

Rules applied to all figures:
- Titles state the finding.
- Text is at least 6.5 pt.
- Orange always means ghost-like and blue always means real.

## 4. Text

- **New section "Data and how we flag a ghost-like name"** before Results. It gives the data, defines the house, states the rule and its checks.
- **Results follow three steps.** Each opens with its conclusion:
  1. Who is on the playlists.
  2. The credits show hundreds of artists; the codes show eight houses.
  3. The recommender mirrors the playlists.

  "What a listener sees" follows as a supporting piece.
- **The two null models are explained in plain words** as "shuffle 1" and "shuffle 2", both in the text and in Fig. 4B.
- **Covers (8,056) and 30-second previews** are now introduced in the data section.
- **Wording.** "Swedish-issued codes" (E8). Same-house links are described without claiming a cause.
- **Epidemic Sound is reframed (E5).** Its own names are correctly not flagged: they have bios and credited composers.
- **Genre playlists (E4).** States that 14 of 493 names are flagged, all real musicians with thin profiles.
- **Abstract (E3, E6)** rewritten from the final numbers, one number per claim.
- **Limitations** are shorter: market, flag errors, Deezer coverage, house size counted within the sample.
